# Frameworks

## Volume: 3 posts a day

1. **Theme post** — follows the weekly rotation below. Usually a thread (5–7 tweets).
2. **"Just the thing"** — a screenshot or short recording plus one line. No thread,
   no explanation. The artefact does the work.
3. **Quick value** — one AI term explained in plain English, a tool pick, or a
   workflow tip. Standalone tweet, occasionally 2–3.

Once leads come in, post them as social proof (with permission, anonymised if
needed). Build in public.

## Weekly rotation (theme post)

| Day | Slot |
|-----|------|
| Mon | A real struggle → the shift → the insight |
| Tue | What I'm building — feature, workflow, screenshot, behind the scenes |
| Wed | Teach one part of my process, or how I think about a problem |
| Thu | A trending topic + my take |
| Fri | Something that didn't work — tried, failed, fixed |
| Sat | One real day. Discipline, consistency, building from Ibadan |
| Sun | Recap — what I built, what I learned, what's next |

## Post skeleton

1. **Hook** — one standalone line. Specific. A number or a tension. It has to work
   as the only thing someone reads.
2. **Context** — 1–2 lines. What this is and why it matters.
3. **Substance** — 3–5 short points. In a thread, one idea per tweet.
4. **Proof** — screenshot or recording on tweet 1 or 2.
5. **Close** — the lesson, then a question or a soft CTA
   ("building something like this? DMs open").

## X rules

- Short lines. Break them up. White space is doing work.
- No hashtag spam.
- Links go in a reply, never the main tweet.
- The first tweet carries the visual.
- A thread's last tweet is where the CTA lives, not the first.

## Content lanes

What this account is about:

- **AI in plain English** — explain a term (agent, tool calling, system prompt,
  RAG, context window) *through something he built*, not as a generic explainer.
- **Tool picks** — better or cheaper options, occasionally free ones. Occasional,
  not the main theme.
- **Build breakdowns** — real projects, process, decisions, what broke. This is
  the client and startup magnet. Lean here.
- **Workflow tips** — how he actually uses AI agents day to day.

Not software-dev tutorials. Not design content.

## Who it's for

Founders and startups who might need a technical person, and regular people who
can't spend much and want more out of AI. Write so both can follow it — the
founder should think "this person could build my thing", the beginner should
think "I finally get what that word means".
