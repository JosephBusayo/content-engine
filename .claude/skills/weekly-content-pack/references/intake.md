# Intake

What's needed each week. If any of it is missing, ask — don't fill the gap.

## Required

1. **Project** — GitHub repo and/or live URL. May be an older project.
2. **Summary, 3–4 lines** — who it's for, the problem it solves, status
   (client / hackathon / personal), and anything confidential.
3. **Demo login** — test account only, if the live site needs one.

## Story bits (only he knows these)

4. **How it started** — what made him build it.
5. **The hardest part** — the thing that took longest or nearly didn't work.
6. **What broke** — a real failure. The repo may hint at it; he confirms it.
7. **Results** — users, revenue, a placement, a client won. If there are none,
   that's an answer. Never manufacture one.
8. **A real detail from his week** — needed for Sat ("one real day"). Where he
   worked, what time, what went wrong, what he ate, the power situation. Small
   and true beats big and vague.

## His take (ask AFTER reading the repo, never before)

Facts come from the repo. The opinion has to come from him, and it's what makes
a post worth reading. Build these questions from what the research turned up so
they're specific, and tell him one-line answers are fine.

9.  **What he actually believes** about this project or how people build things
    like it. What he'd argue with someone about.
10. **A decision he'd defend** if a reviewer questioned it.
11. **What annoyed him** - tedious, stupid, or far slower than it should've been.
12. **What he'd tell a beginner** about it, in one sentence.
13. **What he'd do differently** with another week.
14. **Who he wants reading it** this week - founders, devs, beginners.
15. **What he's tired of hearing** people say about this topic.

Play the angle back to him in 3-4 lines and let him correct it before drafting.

## Confidentiality check

Before drafting, confirm: client name usable? Revenue figures usable? Any
collaborator who shouldn't be named? Any credential visible in a screenshot?

## Then

Append everything confirmed to `projects/<name>/facts.md` with the date, so he's
never asked the same question twice.
